How to Set Up Your Google Business Profile So Phoenix Customers Can Find Your Restaurant

Cómo configurar tu perfil de Google para que te encuentren en Phoenix

Una guía práctica para dueños de restaurantes en Phoenix y Arizona que quieren que su perfil de Google muestre la información correcta: horario, fotos, menú, enlace de pedidos y respuestas a reseñas.

November 3, 2026 · Local marketing

When someone in Phoenix searches "tacos near me" or "breakfast in Mesa," the first thing they usually see is not a website. It is a map with a short list of restaurants, each with photos, hours, and reviews. That information comes from Google Business Profile. If yours is incomplete or out of date, a hungry customer may scroll right past you. The good news: you can fix most of it yourself in an afternoon.

Step 1: Claim and verify your profile

Search for your restaurant's name on Google Maps. If a listing already exists, look for the option to claim or manage it. If nothing shows up, you can create a new profile at business.google.com.

Google will ask you to verify that you really run the business. This can happen by phone, email, video, or mail, depending on what Google offers you. Use an email account the restaurant controls, not one that belongs to a former manager or a cousin who set it up years ago. If you ever lose access, getting it back can be a headache.

Consejo: usa un correo del restaurante que siempre vas a controlar.

Step 2: Get your basic details exactly right

These details matter most, and they should match your website, Facebook, Instagram, and ordering page word for word:

  • Name. Your real restaurant name, nothing extra. Do not stuff keywords like "Best Mexican Food Phoenix" into the name.
  • Address. Double-check suite numbers and the pin on the map, especially in strip malls and plazas.
  • Phone. A number that someone actually answers during business hours.
  • Website and ordering link. Link to your website, and add your commission-free online ordering link for pickup so customers can order straight from you.
  • Menu. Add your menu link or upload your menu so people can see what you serve before they visit.

Step 3: Choose the right categories

Your primary category tells Google what kind of restaurant you are. Pick the most specific one that fits, such as "Mexican restaurant," "Pizza restaurant," or "Breakfast restaurant," rather than just "Restaurant." Then add a few secondary categories that are honestly true, like "Taco restaurant" or "Caterer" if you offer catering. Fewer accurate categories beat a long list of loose ones.

Step 4: Set your hours, including the hard days

Wrong hours are one of the fastest ways to lose a customer's trust. Set your regular hours, then use the special hours feature for holidays, monsoon-season closures, and days you close early for an event. Arizona doesn't change clocks for daylight saving time, but some of your tools and apps may still get confused, so check that your hours display correctly after any update.

Mantén tu horario al día, sobre todo en días festivos.

Step 5: Add photos people want to see

Photos help customers decide where to eat. Upload:

  • Your best-selling dishes, well lit and close up
  • The outside of your building, so people recognize it when they pull in
  • The inside, the counter, and the dining area
  • Your team, if they are comfortable with it

Simple phone photos taken near a window are a good start. AI tools can help brighten or clean up a photo, but the picture should always show your real food as it is served. Add new photos every so often, especially when you launch a new item. Our short lesson on improving a dish photo with AI shows how.

Step 6: Use attributes to answer common questions

Attributes are the small labels on your profile, such as "Dine-in," "Takeout," "Outdoor seating," "Wheelchair accessible," or "Good for kids." Go through the list and turn on every one that is true for your restaurant. If you have your own drivers, you can mark delivery; if not, leave it off. Attributes answer questions before customers have to call.

Step 7: Post updates regularly

Google Business Profile lets you publish short posts with a photo and a button. Use them for:

  • A weekly special or new menu item
  • Holiday hours
  • A reminder that guests can order ahead for pickup from your own page

Keep posts short and friendly. Posting on a simple routine, like once a week, keeps your profile looking active and gives regulars a reason to check back. You can write posts in English and Spanish if your customers use both.

Step 8: Reply to every review

Reviews are a conversation, and other customers read your replies. A few guidelines:

  • Thank people for good reviews, and mention something specific they liked.
  • Stay calm with negative reviews. Apologize for the experience, keep it short, and invite them to contact you directly to make it right.
  • Never argue in public, and never share private details about a customer.
  • Reply in the language of the review. If someone writes in Spanish, answer in Spanish.

AI tools like ChatGPT can help you draft replies faster. Read every draft before posting and make it sound like you.

Responde cada reseña con respeto, en el idioma del cliente.

Step 9: Look at your profile like a customer

Once a month, search for your restaurant on your phone the way a new customer would. Check what Google shows: the hours, the photos at the top, the menu, the ordering link, and any questions or suggested edits from the public. Anyone can suggest changes to a listing, so fix anything that is wrong and keep the details you control up to date. Common things customers want to know: "Do you have vegetarian options?" "Is there parking?" "Can I order ahead?"

Takeaway

Your Google Business Profile is often a customer's first impression of your restaurant. Claim it, keep your hours, photos, and ordering link accurate, post once a week, and reply to every review. Small, steady updates make a big difference.

En resumen: tu perfil de Google es la primera impresión. Mantenlo al día, publica cada semana y responde cada reseña.

A simple weekly checklist

  • Check that hours are correct for the coming week
  • Reply to any new reviews
  • Answer new questions
  • Add one post (a special, a dish, or a pickup reminder)
  • Upload a new photo when you have one
